# Bleach Bypass

## Definition
A desaturated, contrasty film-processing look — built in this course via a [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Layer Mixer]] with a **Soft Light** Composite Mode, blending a fully desaturated version of the image back with the color primaries underneath at partial strength.

## In Practice
A genuinely instructive live self-correction in this course: reaching for a [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Parallel Node]] first, then catching the mistake and switching to a layer mixer instead — a parallel node blends corrections additively with no blend-mode concept, while a true bleach-bypass effect specifically requires a layer mixer's blend-mode control. The two tools aren't interchangeable for this particular effect, even though they can look similar in other contexts.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Skipping a step in film development on purpose, for the "damage"**: the technique's name comes from literally skipping (or partially skipping) the bleaching step in traditional film processing, which normally removes silver from the print — leaving some silver in produces a desaturated, high-contrast, gritty look. Digital emulation recreates that same visual signature (pushed contrast, pulled saturation) without touching any actual chemistry.
