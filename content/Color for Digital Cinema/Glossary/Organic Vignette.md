# Organic Vignette

## Definition
A power-window vignette shaped to follow a shot's actual compositional lines (a road, a doorway, a subject's silhouette) rather than a generic centered oval — reads as unintentional/natural rather than an obviously placed effect.

## In Practice
The backbone technique behind most of this course's eye-direction demos: trace an existing line in the frame with the [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Power Windows|Power Curve]] tool, soften heavily, brighten the selected area while keeping shadows low (see [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Primary Wheels (Lift, Gamma, Gain, Offset)]]), then add an [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Outside Node]] to darken/desaturate everything else. Restated as a closing principle late in the course: vignettes should be built per-shot, following that shot's own lines, never applied as one uniform global treatment reused across every clip.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A candle's glow vs. a flashlight's circle**: a standard vignette is a clean, uniform, obviously artificial darkening toward the frame edges. An Organic Vignette is built to feel uneven and natural — closer to how real light actually falls off in a room — so it reads as atmosphere rather than as a filter someone applied.
