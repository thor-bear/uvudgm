# Tint

## Definition
The green/magenta color axis — accessed via the control panel's Offset button alongside [[Temperature]], on the adjacent wheel.

## In Practice
Particularly relevant for correcting mixed/bad lighting sources — fluorescents commonly cast green or magenta depending on bulb type. Practical framing from this course: untrained eyes tend to tolerate a magenta cast more readily than a green one, but a trained eye should treat both as equally wrong — the goal is a neutral middle point, not overcorrecting one cast into the opposite cast. Stylistic green/magenta pushes (the "David Fincher" look, as one example cited) are a separate, intentional creative choice, distinct from an unintentional lighting-cast problem.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Temperature's perpendicular partner**: if Temperature is the warm-cool axis (orange to blue), Tint is the axis running crosswise to it (green to magenta) — together they form a full compass for correcting any lighting cast, not just the obvious warm/cool ones. Fluorescent lighting is the classic Tint problem, since it commonly pushes green or magenta rather than warm or cool.
- Worth stating the trained-eye point directly: most people's eyes tolerate a magenta cast more easily than a green one, but both are equally "wrong" from a neutral-balance standpoint — the goal is landing in the middle, not swapping one cast for its opposite.
