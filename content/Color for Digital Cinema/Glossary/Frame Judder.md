# Frame Judder

## Definition
A visible artifact from mismatching a timeline's frame rate to the actual frame rate of its footage — duplicated frames create an uneven, stuttering cadence rather than smooth motion.

## In Practice
This course's explicit warning: match the timeline frame rate to whatever the majority of your footage actually is, and treat frame judder as acceptable only when a specific broadcast delivery spec genuinely requires the mismatch. Comes up as part of the standing project-settings checklist alongside resolution and color management defaults — a symptom worth checking for early, since a wrong frame rate doesn't announce itself the way a wrong resolution does.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Watching a flipbook with a few pages taped together in the wrong spots**: when a timeline's frame rate doesn't match the footage's actual frame rate, some frames get duplicated to make the math work out — instead of smooth, even motion, playback develops a subtle stutter, the visual equivalent of a flipbook where a couple of pages got stuck together and skip the eye's expectation of even pacing.
