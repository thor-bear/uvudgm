# Parade (Scope)

## Definition
Functionally the same as [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Waveform (Scope)]] but splits R/G/B into three independent traces — generally preferred over plain waveform for spotting channel-specific imbalances (e.g. one channel sitting lower than the others).

## In Practice
The single clearest diagnostic used throughout this course for judging white balance: a well-balanced shot shows its three channels roughly aligned (not identical — that would mean a black-and-white image — but close, with deviation explained by a real scene element like a blue sky). This course's default two-view scope setup pairs Parade with [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Vectorscope]] over the default Waveform/Histogram combination.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Three side-by-side thermometers, one per color**: instead of one combined reading, the Parade shows Red, Green, and Blue as three separate graphs standing next to each other — if all three roughly agree, the image is neutral; if one runs noticeably hotter or colder than the others, that's your color cast, visible as a number instead of a guess.
- Good live demo: point at a shot with an obvious color cast, ask a student to guess the cast by eye, then reveal the Parade and show the misaligned channel — makes the case for scopes over eyeballing better than just asserting it.
