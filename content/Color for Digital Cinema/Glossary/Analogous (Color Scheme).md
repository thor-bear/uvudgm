# Analogous (Color Scheme)

## Definition
Colors adjacent to each other on the wheel — a "softer" cousin of monochromatic (related hues rather than one identical hue).

## In Practice
Wes Anderson is cited in this course as a filmmaker who leans on this scheme frequently, with examples including yellow/orange/green/blue groupings and pink/purple/blue groupings.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Neighbors on the color wheel, not opposites**: pick any three or four colors sitting right next to each other on the wheel (yellow, orange, red) and you get a palette that feels cohesive and gentle rather than punchy — the softer, quieter cousin of Monochromatic (one hue) sitting between it and Complementary (opposite hues) in terms of visual tension.
- Wes Anderson's palettes are worth keeping as the go-to reference — showing a still from one of his films and asking students to name the 3-4 adjacent hues at work is a fast way to make the concept concrete.
