# High Key/Low Key (Lighting)

## Definition
A reminder that color meaning isn't just about hue — how bright or dark an image is (luminosity) matters independently and compounds with color choices. **High key**: bright, balanced, natural. **Low key**: dark, gritty.

## In Practice
Demonstrated in this course with side-by-side high-key and low-key versions of the same conceptual shot, then illustrating that the same bright image could be recolored (e.g. pushed yellow) to shift its feeling without changing its brightness at all — luminosity and hue are separate, stackable levers a colorist can pull independently.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **The room's overall lighting mood, independent of what color the paint is**: a room can be painted any color and still be lit brightly and evenly (High Key) or moodily with deep shadows (Low Key) — brightness/contrast structure and hue are two completely separate levers, and this term is specifically about the first one.
- Good demo already built into this course: take the same bright, balanced shot and recolor it (pushed yellow) without changing its brightness at all — proves the two axes are genuinely independent rather than just conceptually distinct.
