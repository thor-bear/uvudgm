# Aspect Ratio (Cinema, Delivery Standards)

## Definition
Cameras frequently shoot in a larger or differently-shaped format than the intended final delivery, deliberately, for reframing flexibility in post. Standard cinema has only two true resolutions: **1.85:1 (Flat)** — 4K: 3996×2160, 2K: 1998×1080 — and **2.39:1 (Scope)**, not 2.35:1 which is an obsolete 1970s standard — 4K: 4096×1716, 2K: 2048×858.

## In Practice
Standard TV is **16:9 only** — anything else gets letterboxed/pillarboxed, except for web delivery, which crops black bars entirely rather than letterboxing. Non-standard but genuinely common creative formats: **2:1 (Univisium)** — revived by *Jurassic World* (2015), now widely used across prestige streaming (*Stranger Things*); **1.33:1 (4:3)**, the old TV standard used occasionally for stylistic effect; **1.66:1**, an older cinema standard occasionally revived for nostalgic purposes. Creative aspect ratio choice is unrestricted, but *delivery* to a specific channel (DCP, broadcast) requires conforming to that channel's accepted standard via pillarboxing/letterboxing, planned for in advance. See [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/DCP (Digital Cinema Package)]] for delivery-specific resolution requirements.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Choosing between a widescreen photo print and a square one**: aspect ratio is just the shape of the frame — width relative to height — and different delivery contexts expect different shapes (2.39:1 for a cinema scope image, 1.85:1 for standard theatrical, 16:9 for broadcast/streaming). Deliver the wrong shape and the image either gets awkwardly cropped or shows unwanted bars, the same way printing a widescreen photo onto square paper forces an unwanted choice.
