# White Balance

## Definition
The state where highlights, shadows, and midtones all sit at a neutral color temperature — white reads as white, black as black, with no color cast in any direction. One of the core checkpoints for what "done" looks like after primary corrections.

## In Practice
A poorly white-balanced image (e.g. everything tinted orange from warm lighting) makes later selections much harder — skin tones, a blue shirt, any specific object become difficult to key because the global tint has collapsed their distinctness. Balancing first makes every subsequent secondary/targeted adjustment easier and cleaner. Diagnosed most directly on the [[Parade (Scope)]]: a well-balanced shot shows its three channels roughly aligned; a visibly misaligned trace is the clearest sign of an unbalanced shot. Also correctable directly via the Color Temperature slider in the Camera RAW panel, equivalent to adjusting white balance in-camera.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Cleaning a pair of tinted sunglasses**: if your glasses have an orange tint, everything you look at reads slightly orange — white balancing is wiping that tint off the lens so white actually looks white again, before you decide to add any color back on purpose.
- Quick classroom test: ask a student to point at the whitest object in a poorly-balanced frame and describe its actual color out loud ("it's kind of orange-ish white") — naming the cast out loud is often the fastest way to get someone who "doesn't see it yet" to suddenly see it.
