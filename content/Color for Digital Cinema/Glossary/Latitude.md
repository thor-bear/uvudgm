# Latitude

## Definition
How much you can push or recover exposure in post without significantly compromising the image — "the malleability of your image." Related to, but distinct from, [[Dynamic Range]]: a camera can capture a wide dynamic range and still have low latitude if the data isn't robust enough (low bit depth, heavy compression) to survive aggressive grading.

## In Practice
The Sony a7S/a7S II is the counterexample cited in this course: high dynamic range on paper (it shoots log) but low actual latitude, because low bit depth and heavy compression mean the data can't survive aggressive grading even though the range was technically captured. Latitude is one of the properties (alongside chroma subsampling, bit depth, and codec) that determines how far primary corrections can push an image before it breaks — see [[Primary Corrections]].

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **How far you can stretch a rubber band before it snaps**: Latitude is that stretchiness — how aggressively you can push exposure, shadows, and highlights around before the image starts falling apart (banding, crushed blacks, blown-out highlights with nothing left to recover). High-latitude formats (RAW, Log) stretch a long way before snapping; low-latitude formats snap much sooner.
