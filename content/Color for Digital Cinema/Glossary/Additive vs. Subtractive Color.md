# Additive vs. Subtractive Color

## Definition
- **Additive** (light): primaries are Red, Green, Blue — combining more color makes the result *brighter*. This is how DaVinci Resolve fundamentally works, and how displays work.
- **Subtractive** (physical pigment/dye — paint, ink, film): primaries are Cyan, Magenta, Yellow — combining more color makes the result *darker*, trending toward brown/black.

## In Practice
"Red, yellow, blue" as commonly taught is not accurate for either system — a simplification for children who don't yet have vocabulary for cyan/magenta. Film is a physical, subtractive medium, meaning authentic film emulation in Resolve (an additive-color tool) is necessarily a *simulation* of subtractive behavior, not the same underlying math — the conceptual basis for why [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Technicolor (2-Strip, 3-Strip Emulation)]] emulation requires an actual RGB→CMY channel conversion, not just a color tint.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A dark room with colored spotlights vs. a white page with colored inks**: Additive color (RGB, how screens work) starts from black and *adds* light — combine all three and you get white. Subtractive color (CMY, how printing/paint works) starts from white and *removes* light by absorbing it — combine all three pigments and you approach black. Screens add light; print subtracts it.
