# Look (Color Grade)

## Definition
The overall style and color scheme applied to a film — added *after* primary corrections, and after or alongside secondaries (secondaries are frequently the actual building blocks a look is constructed from).

## In Practice
Core distinction stressed throughout this course: primary corrections are not subjective — footage is either balanced or it isn't; **looks are subjective**, the space where creative, authorial choice lives. A look is something the author defines, not the camera/medium — relying on a camera's native color science as your "look" without further grading is treated as skipping the creative pipeline, not using it. Tools for building a look named across this course: color schemes, limiting/consolidating a palette, contrast, midtone detail, sharpness/softness, mist/glow, film emulation, grain, halation. **Critical reusable technique**: a look built on one shot transfers to others only if primaries already match across shots — temporarily delete the primary node(s), grab a Still of the now-look-only chain, immediately Undo to restore primaries, then Append Node Graph (not middle-click) on other shots to add just the look on top of their own primaries.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **The outfit, not the person underneath**: a "Look" is the intentional creative style layered on top of an already-balanced, well-primaried image — the person (the primaried footage) needs to be healthy and well-proportioned before you dress them in a specific style; putting the outfit on a poorly-balanced foundation is what produces those unconvincing "attempted style with nothing underneath" examples this course already uses.
