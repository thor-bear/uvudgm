# DIT (Digital Imaging Technician)

## Definition
More than "the person who backs up footage" — a common misconception, especially on lower-budget productions. The connective link between production and post-production: ensuring the on-set image gets to post correctly, keeping monitors calibrated/matched, potentially building a [[Show LUT (Pre-Production LUT)]] with the DP to maintain a consistent style through the pipeline. Data management/backup remains the first-priority responsibility regardless of what else is asked of the role.

## In Practice
Colorists and DITs often share the same core skillset (color literacy, file organization, data management) — on independent films they're frequently the same person, or should at least be interchangeable. "Dailies colorist" is the more formal term for someone doing preliminary color specifically, sometimes working on-set during production itself, ahead of any post-production colorist involvement.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **The on-set data librarian**: while a script supervisor tracks continuity and a colorist grades in post, the DIT is the person on set making sure every card gets safely offloaded, backed up, and verified before anyone wipes anything — the person standing between "we shot it" and "we might have actually lost it," working in real time under production pressure.
