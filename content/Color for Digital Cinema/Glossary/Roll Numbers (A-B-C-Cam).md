# Roll Numbers (A/B/C-Cam)

## Definition
Camera media organized by day, then by roll number, with a letter prefix per camera (A = A-cam, B = B-cam, C = a third camera/drone/GoPro, etc.). Roll numbers increment per mag/card change, but reset to 1 only per *film*, not per day — a long shoot might reach roll 60–90 per camera by the end.

## In Practice
The 2nd AC is responsible for keeping roll numbers correctly updated on both the camera mag and the slate; the DIT (as part of the camera team) can and should flag or correct this if it's being done wrong, since downstream organization depends on it. Not all cameras support proper roll-number resets — for non-resettable cameras, the DIT just assigns roll numbers manually within the folder structure. Sound gets its own parallel **A-roll numbering** (A001, A002, etc.) unless B/C cameras genuinely have separate audio sources, so audio numbering directly correlates with the corresponding video roll.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Labeling which of several identical suitcases belongs to which traveler**: on a multicam shoot, A-Cam, B-Cam, and C-Cam are just consistent labels for "the primary angle," "the second angle," and so on — a simple naming convention that keeps footage from multiple simultaneous cameras from becoming an unsorted pile once everything lands in one project.
