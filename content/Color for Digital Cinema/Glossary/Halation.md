# Halation

## Definition
A warm/orange glow specifically at high-contrast edges, inherent to physical film stock itself — not something a lens does. One of the named building blocks of "the film look" in this course.

## In Practice
Comes up as part of the vocabulary for articulating a vague "cinematic" request into specific, actionable technical choices — alongside softness, grain size, and highlight rolloff. Not deeply demonstrated as its own standalone technique in this course, but named as a real, physically-grounded characteristic worth knowing when discussing film emulation with a director or DP.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A halo of light bleeding backward through the film itself**: bright light sources (streetlights at night, a window) physically scatter and bounce off the back of the film base before exposing it, producing a soft reddish glow that spills outward from the source — like a drop of red dye slowly spreading outward in water from a bright point of light.
