# RGB Mixer

## Definition
A tool (icon next to the color wheels) that pulls information from one output channel into another — e.g. pulling red and green information *into* the blue output channel if blue is contaminated or underexposed.

## In Practice
Its single best real-world use case, stated directly in this course: rescuing a channel that's been crushed or underexposed relative to the others — not general color grading. Watch the [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Parade (Scope)]] directly while adjusting; it's a "balance by ear" tool more than a formula. Overshooting the correction needs to be dialed back and rebalanced with a touch of the original channel added back in — treated as a normal, expected iterative process. After the mixer gets an image roughly neutral, standard primaries/curves finish the look.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A cross-fader board for the three color channels**: instead of adjusting Red, Green, and Blue only within their own lanes, RGB Mixer lets you blend a percentage of one channel into another — like routing a bit of the bass track into the vocal channel on a mixing board — useful for fixing weird channel-specific color casts that a normal wheel or curve can't reach.
