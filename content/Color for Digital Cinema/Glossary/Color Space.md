# Color Space

## Definition
A digital system for organizing and representing color, designed to reproduce what human vision perceives within the technical limits of a given display or format. Visualized as a horseshoe-shaped diagram of total human color perception, with individual color spaces shown as triangles occupying different portions of it — some spaces are even wider than human vision, used for archival/mastering headroom rather than display.

## In Practice
Every color space is really two properties bundled together: **gamut** (how wide a range of hues it covers) and **gamma** (its contrast curve / brightness handling). The two get confused constantly, but they're independent axes — see the separate Gamut and Gamma entries. Rec. 709, P3-DCI, and Rec. 2020 are the three color spaces this course compares directly. Covered in more depth in the Color Terms Study Guide's Color Space Fundamentals section.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A box of crayons vs. a full pigment mixing set**: a color space defines which colors are even available to work with — a smaller box (Rec. 709) has fewer, more limited crayons; a bigger box (Rec. 2020) has a wider range of possible colors to draw from, even if the image itself never actually uses the extras.
- Useful to distinguish from Gamut directly: Color Space is the whole system (which colors, how bright, how contrast maps) — Gamut is specifically the "which colors" part of that system, the boundary of what's possible to represent at all.
