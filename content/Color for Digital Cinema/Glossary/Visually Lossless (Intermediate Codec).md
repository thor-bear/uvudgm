# Visually Lossless (Intermediate Codec)

## Definition
One of three codec categories covered in this course: ProRes, DNxHR, DPX, EXR, TIFF. Low compression or uncompressed, high bitrate, high bit depth (typically 10/12-bit+), chroma subsampling no worse than 4:2:2 (often 4:4:4:4).

## In Practice
Used for camera recording, intermediate work (color, VFX), and film mastering. "Visually lossless" specifically means the eye can't perceive the loss at normal viewing — a real, if usually imperceptible, difference still exists, most visible when pixel-peeping or converting between subsampling levels. [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/ProRes (Codec Family)]] is described in this course as the de facto industry standard for this category.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A photocopy so good you can't tell it from the original**: technically some data is discarded in compression, but not enough for the human eye to detect any difference under normal viewing — "visually lossless" is a practical compromise between a true lossless file's huge size and a heavily compressed file's visible quality loss.
