# Reference Display (Calibrated Monitor)

## Definition
A calibrated display used to judge color accurately — distinct from an ordinary, uncalibrated GUI monitor used just for clicking around Resolve's interface. Three things to check, roughly in priority order: **10-bit color depth** (many budget screens are 8-bit and simply don't advertise it — silence on the spec is itself a signal), **full Rec. 709 gamut coverage** (not guaranteed even on some consumer screens), and **4K resolution** (least important of the three).

## In Practice
True professional-grade references (Sony BVM-X300, now discontinued and succeeded by the BVM-HX3110; Dolby Pulsar; Flanders Scientific) run $20–50K+, with some now discontinued due to limited market demand at that price point. This course's most accessible real-world recommendation: a calibrated consumer **LG OLED TV** (~$2,000, colorimeter probe + Calman Home software ~$150) — genuinely used as *client* monitors at real color houses, and cited in this course as good enough that colorist Joel Bogdanovich reportedly graded an entire season of Netflix's *The Umbrella Academy* on one while working from home. For HDR specifically, OLED's self-emissive pixels (true per-pixel black, no bleed) make it the best consumer-accessible technology; high-end backlit LCDs with many independently-dimmable zones (Apple Pro Display XDR, Flanders) approximate the same effect without matching it exactly.

*Note: hardware prices in this note reflect figures stated in the course and general market range as of a mid-2026 check — verify current pricing directly with the manufacturer before making a purchase decision.*

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A tuned reference speaker vs. a car stereo**: a car stereo is voiced to sound exciting, boosting bass and treble in ways that would ruin a mix if you trusted it while mastering audio. An uncalibrated monitor does the same thing to color — it can look great and still be lying to you, which is exactly why the images being graded on it need a display that's been told to just show the truth.
- **A scale that reads five pounds light**: if a bathroom scale is off, every decision made from it is off in the same direction without you knowing it. An uncalibrated display shifts every color decision made on it, silently, in whatever direction that display happens to be biased.
