# Vectorscope

## Definition
Shows hue and saturation only (no luminance) — plotted against a color wheel matching the app's hue wheel. A fully desaturated image collapses to a single dot at center. The "boxes" around the wheel edge represent broadcast-legal saturation limits (see [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Broadcast Legalization (Broadcast Safe)]]).

## In Practice
A **skin-tone indicator** line can be toggled on in scope settings — a rough guide for where skin should land hue-wise on the vectorscope, regardless of the person's actual skin tone (skin hue is fairly consistent across people; only the luminance value shifts with complexion). Explicit caveat: it's a loose guide, not a target to force-match — a naturally paler or flushed complexion, or intentionally stylized lighting, can and should deviate from it. Also the diagnostic used for reading a reference film's color-scheme logic when reverse-engineering a look — see [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Look Matching via Scopes (Reference Footage Analysis)]].

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A compass for color, not brightness**: the Vectorscope doesn't care how bright or dark something is — only which direction (hue) and how far from center (saturation) it points. A dot near the middle is desaturated/gray; a dot pushed way out toward "R" is a strongly saturated red.
- The built-in skin-tone line is worth demonstrating directly: put a face on screen, point at where its dot lands relative to that line, and let students see for themselves why it's used as a fast, repeatable check rather than eyeballing whether skin "looks right."
