# Color Bible

## Definition
2–3 representative shots pulled from every scene in a film, graded first as a reference set and approved by the DP/director before full-scene grading begins.

## In Practice
Cited in this course from the *King Kong* (2005) BTS material (David Cole, colorist) — directly analogous to this course's own shot-matching-via-stills technique, just scaled up to feature-film production. See [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Look Matching via Scopes (Reference Footage Analysis)]] and [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Shot-Matching]] for the classroom-scale versions of the same underlying idea.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A production's own style guide**: the same way a brand keeps a document defining its exact logo colors, fonts, and spacing rules, a Color Bible documents a film or show's intended color scheme, tonal targets, and per-scene/per-character color logic — so every colorist working on the project (and every episode of a series) stays consistent with the same intentional plan, rather than everyone improvising independently.
