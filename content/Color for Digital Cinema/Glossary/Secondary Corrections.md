# Secondary Corrections

## Definition
Targeted, selective adjustments — anything keyed, masked, tracked, or otherwise limited to part of the image. This is where a "look" gets built (split toning, bleach bypass, film grain, heavy skin smoothing, etc.). Maps onto the general industry term "color grading," as distinct from "color correction" (primaries/technical normalization).

## In Practice
Often described with a surgeon metaphor: primaries prep the patient/table, secondaries are picking up the scalpel — more technical, more precise, more capable of real harm if handled carelessly. In real freelance work, secondaries and "the look" are frequently inseparable — secondaries are often *how* a look gets built. Core secondary techniques covered across this course: [[HSL Qualifier]], [[Power Windows]], [[Motion Tracking]], [[Dodge and Burn]], and node types like [[Outside Node]] and [[Layer Mixer]].

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Highlighting one sentence in an otherwise-finished document**: primaries fix the whole page; secondaries reach in and adjust just one specific word or phrase — a shirt, a sky, a face — without touching anything around it.
- The dependency is worth stating as a rule of thumb: secondaries built on a poorly-primaried shot are like trying to highlight a sentence in a document with inconsistent, blurry text — the selection tools have a much harder time finding a clean edge to grab onto.
