# Dynamic Range

## Definition
The total range from darkest shadow to brightest highlight a camera can capture in a single shot, measured in stops. The ARRI Alexa 35 is cited in this course as having the highest dynamic range of any digital camera sensor to date (~17.5 stops), making it very hard to clip highlights or crush shadows even in difficult lighting.

## In Practice
Frequently confused with [[Latitude]], which is related but distinct: dynamic range is what the sensor *captured*, latitude is how much you can *push/recover* that footage in post without breaking it. A camera can have high dynamic range on paper but low actual latitude if its bit depth or compression can't survive aggressive grading — the Sony a7S/a7S II is cited in this course as exactly this case.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **The distance between the darkest whisper and the loudest shout a system can capture**: dynamic range is that whole span, from the deepest recoverable shadow to the brightest recoverable highlight — a camera/format with more dynamic range can capture a quieter whisper and a louder shout in the same take without either one getting clipped off.
- Directly tie to Latitude to avoid conflating the two: Dynamic Range is how wide the range *is*; Latitude is how much you can *push* the footage around within that range before it falls apart — related, but answering different questions.
