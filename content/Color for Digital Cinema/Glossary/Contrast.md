# Contrast

## Definition
The difference between highlights and shadows — adjustable via a dedicated contrast slider, or indirectly via lift/gain. Visible on the Waveform scope: cranking contrast creates a visible S-curve shape (the origin of the term "S-curve"). Paired with [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Pivot]], the point that divides "highlight" from "shadow" when applying contrast.

## In Practice
This course draws a distinction between "color-contrasted" (distinct hues stay distinct, no wash muddying the frame) and "luma-contrasted" (normal real-world brightness range — bright highlights, dark shadows, correctly-placed midtones) as two separate things a well-graded image needs. A recurring technique for deepening contrast without crushing shadows below zero on the scope: use [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Curves (Custom, RGB Curves)]] rather than Lift, anchoring the curve's low end in place while pulling down specifically the lower-midtone region.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Stretching a rubber band around a fixed center point**: increasing Contrast pushes shadows darker and highlights brighter simultaneously, stretching the tonal range outward — the Pivot point is where that rubber band is anchored, the point that stays put while everything else stretches away from it.
- The "real-world eyes as your reference" tip already used elsewhere in this course applies directly here: ask a student how much contrast they actually perceive on a face standing in normal daylight, and use that felt sense as the target rather than an arbitrary slider position.
