# Saturation

## Definition
The intensity of a hue. The same hue at different saturation levels reads as genuinely different colors perceptually (e.g. forest green vs. bright green — same hue, different saturation).

## In Practice
Human eyes are drawn to saturated things and comparatively uninterested in desaturated ones — a recurring eye-direction principle: if a saturated object is distracting, desaturate it rather than removing it. A common gotcha worth remembering: desaturating a very saturated color can make it read as *darker* even though luminance didn't technically change (a perceptual effect), sometimes needing a small brightness compensation. Read directly on the [[Atlas/Notes/Student Notes/Color for Digital Cinema/Glossary/Vectorscope]], where distance from center indicates saturation.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A dimmer switch for color intensity, not brightness**: turning Saturation down slides every color toward gray while brightness stays put; turning it up makes every color more vivid and intense. It's a completely separate axis from Luminance — you can have a bright, desaturated image or a dark, highly saturated one.
- The "vibrant but not stylized" target already used in this course's Primaries lesson is worth repeating here directly: enough saturation that later keying work is feasible, not so much that the shot reads as an intentional stylistic push before any style has actually been decided on.
