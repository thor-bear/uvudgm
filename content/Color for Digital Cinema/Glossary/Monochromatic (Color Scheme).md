# Monochromatic (Color Scheme)

## Definition
One of four color schemes covered in this course: the same hue throughout an image at varying luminance/saturation.

## In Practice
Cited examples: interior-design photography with a single dominant hue set-designed in (not color-graded), and the film *Her*, built around a nearly single-hue palette despite being full color. Color schemes in general are framed with a stated purpose: creating harmonious, intentional-feeling images rather than ones that read as visually random — and are explicitly not limited to the grading stage, since they get built in production design and wardrobe as much as, or more than, in the grade.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **One paint color, every shade on the can's swatch card**: Monochromatic sticks to a single hue but varies its brightness and saturation freely — like decorating an entire room in different shades and intensities of blue, never introducing a second hue. *Her* is worth keeping as the reference: full color, but built almost entirely around one dominant hue family.
