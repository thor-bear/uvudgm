# Control Surface (Grading Panel)

## Definition
A dedicated hardware panel for grading — trackballs/wheels and buttons that map directly to Resolve's color tools, faster and more tactile than mouse-driven grading.

## In Practice
This course's class panel is the DaVinci Resolve Micro Panel; the instructor's own recommendation for best value beyond that is the **Resolve Mini Panel** (~$2,300–2,500 depending on retailer, verified current as of mid-2026), same core layout as the Micro Panel with a fuller top button section. Budget options: the free **Tangent Element iPad app** (no tactile feedback, but functional) or a **Tangent Ripple** (~$300–350 new; the course's cited "~$100 used" figure was the instructor's own number at time of recording and reads low against current market pricing — worth checking current listings rather than relying on that figure). At the top end, Blackmagic's own **Resolve Advanced/Control Surface** (roughly $30,000) is found at real color houses — explicitly framed as "know it exists, don't buy it." Framed throughout as aspirational/informational, not required — this course's own class setup (Micro Panel, uncalibrated displays) is treated as fully adequate for learning.

*Note: hardware prices fluctuate by retailer and region — treat these as ballpark figures, not quotes.*

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A mixing board vs. a mouse doing all the same jobs, slower**: three trackball wheels under three fingers can adjust Lift/Gamma/Gain simultaneously, the way a real mixing console lets a sound engineer ride several faders at once — a mouse can technically do the same adjustments, but only one control at a time, in sequence, the way clicking through a software mixer one channel at a time is technically possible but nowhere near as fluid as physical hands on physical dials.
