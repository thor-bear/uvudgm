# DI (Digital Intermediate)

## Definition
The digital color-finishing stage of a film's pipeline — historically a milestone shift from photochemical grading to digital.

## In Practice
Cited in this course via the *King Kong* (2005) BTS material: the DP (Andrew Lesnie) deliberately overexposed the negative slightly on set, knowing a DI pass was coming, to preserve maximum shadow/highlight information for the colorist to work with later — an early example of "shooting for the DI pipeline" as a deliberate production strategy, distinct from just shooting well-exposed footage. *The Lord of the Rings: Fellowship of the Ring* (2001) is also referenced as one of the first films to receive a full digital grade, roughly 70% of the film passing through digital grading.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **The whole finishing stage, not just one tool inside it**: DI is the umbrella term for the entire digital post-production finishing process a film goes through — color grading is the most visible part, but DI as a term also covers the broader digital pipeline that replaced older photochemical film-finishing processes. Worth distinguishing from "color grading" specifically: grading is a task within the DI process, not a synonym for it.
