# Color Boost

## Definition
A saturation control that targets already low-saturation areas preferentially, rather than boosting saturation uniformly across the whole image the way the standard Saturation slider does.

## In Practice
This course is candid that the exact internal logic of how Color Boost decides what counts as "low saturation" isn't fully understood, and expresses a personal preference for a more controllable approach instead: keying/selecting the specific low-saturation area intended for a boost, then using the standard [[Saturation]] control on just that selection.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **An equalizer that boosts quiet instruments more than loud ones**: standard Saturation turns every color up by the same amount, loud and quiet alike. Color Boost specifically reaches for the already-quiet (low-saturation) parts of the image and boosts those preferentially, leaving already-vibrant colors closer to where they were.
- Worth passing along the instructor's own honest framing here: it's a "smart but slightly opaque" tool — useful, but if you want precise control over exactly *which* low-saturation area gets boosted, keying that specific area and using standard Saturation on the selection is the more transparent alternative.
