# Dodge and Burn

## Definition
Brightening what should be emphasized in a shot and darkening what shouldn't, via power windows — attributed in this course to a reading assignment's discussion of Dan Moran's dodging-and-burning technique.

## In Practice
The throughline for a whole run of eye-direction demos in this course: eyes are drawn to the brightest thing in frame regardless of how much of the frame it occupies, so a power-window brighten-the-subject / darken-everything-else pattern (via an [[Outside Node]]) reliably redirects attention. Works independently of color choice — a scene built in black-and-white (saturation at zero) can still read strongly on contrast-based dodge-and-burn alone, with color reintroduced afterward.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Sculpting light and shadow onto a flat face with a soft brush**: Dodge (brightening) and Burn (darkening) are painted, localized touches — subtly lifting a cheekbone or deepening a jawline's shadow — the digital descendant of a darkroom photographer literally waving their hands over an enlarger to shield or expose parts of a print longer.
