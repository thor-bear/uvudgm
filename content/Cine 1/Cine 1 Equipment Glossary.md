# Cine 1 Equipment Glossary

> [!info] About this glossary
> Definitions were compiled with AI assistance (Claude) to explain the equipment terms introduced on day one of Cine 1. Photos are representative examples; a few (pipe clamp, pigeon plate, chain vise grip, grip clips, dolly & track, jib) are stand-ins rather than the exact model shown in class. Tap a 🔧 entry to expand it.

> [!note]- 🔧 Disconnect box
> The main power source for the whole set — a portable distribution box (often called a "disco" or "bull switch") that takes in three-phase power from a generator or building tie-in and breaks it out into the smaller feeder cables ("pigtails") that run to lights. 400 amps is genuinely dangerous — this is why only the gaffer and electricians ("sparks"/"juicers") are allowed to touch it. [BarnDoor Lighting: distro boxes](https://www.filmandvideolighting.com/electrical-distribution-distro-box-boxes-stage-tv-film-video-lighting.html)
> ![[lex_products_ph400a1_n2j_26bc2cc_400a_cinebox_motion_picture_1597314932_1571230.jpg|Disconnect box]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Pigtails
> Short feeder cables that branch off the disconnect box, carrying power out to distro boxes or directly to large lights. The caps protect the exposed copper camlock connectors when not in use — critical since these carry hundreds of amps. [Film set power distribution guide](https://lightbulbrentals.com/film-set-power-guide/)
> ![[lex_products_fbl305w_50_2_sc_5_wire_banded_1553790027_1463723.jpg|Pigtails]]

> [!note]- 🔧 5000-watt light ("5K")
> A large open-face or fresnel tungsten fixture — one of the workhorse units for lighting big areas or exteriors day-for-night. Nicknamed by wattage the way "blonde" (2K) and "redhead" (1K) are. 5Ks weigh 60+ lbs, so always use a team lift. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[Mole_Richardson_4351_Senior_5K_Tungsten_Fresnel_1232700275_273306.jpg|5000-watt tungsten fresnel]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Barn doors
> The hinged metal flaps on the front of a fixture that fold in to shape and control the spread of the beam, keeping light off places you don't want it (like the lens or a wall). Tungsten bulbs and barn doors get very hot. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[Litepanels_1BD_4_Way_Barndoors_for_1x1_1254233790_644927.jpg|Four-way barn doors]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Silk
> A large diffusion fabric stretched in a frame (a "butterfly") and placed between a light and the subject to soften and spread it. The yellow border is Matthews/Rosco's house color-coding so crews can ID a silk at a glance from across a set. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[cinegearfactory_cgf_la_bdrlf_44_butterfly_diffusion_reflector_lighting_1721148065_1837673.jpg|Butterfly frame with silk]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Net / scrim
> A wire-mesh or fabric screen placed in front of a light to cut its intensity *without* changing its quality or spread the way diffusion does — it just knocks the light down. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[Matthews_149064_Open_End_Scrim_1502985955_32778.jpg|Open-end scrim]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Stop, and net/scrim color coding
> A "stop" is a doubling or halving of light — the same exposure math as an f-stop on a lens. The colored borders on nets are an industry-standard shorthand: green = a "single" (reduces light by about 1/2 stop), red = a "double" (a full stop). Knowing the border color lets a gaffer grab the right net without reading a label.

> [!note]- 🔧 Flag / solid / cutter
> "Flag" is the umbrella term for any rigid light-blocking panel (black fabric stretched over a metal frame). A "solid" or "cutter" blocks light entirely to create a hard-edged shadow or keep light off a specific area (like the lens, to kill flare). [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[Matthews_169062_24x36_Flag_1613493333_32828.jpg|24x36 flag]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Floppy
> A cutter with an extra flap of fabric attached by Velcro that can fold out to roughly double the flag's coverage area — useful when you need a bigger cutter than you brought. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[matthews_169025t_floppy_top_hinge_40_1591015845_1563276.jpg|Floppy]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Cookie (cucoloris)
> A flag with irregular holes cut into it — plywood or foam core — that breaks up a light beam into a dappled, organic pattern, classically used to fake dappled sunlight through leaves. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[kupo_kg089311_wood_cucoloris_cookie_breakup_1728566437_1525247.jpg|Wooden cookie]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Venetian cutter
> A flag with horizontal slats cut into it, used to throw a striped, venetian-blind-style shadow pattern — a common way to fake light coming through window blinds.
> ![[kupo_kg089511_wood_cucoloris_cookie_blinds_1728566728_1525249.jpg|Venetian cutter]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Bazooka
> The center riser column of a C-stand or similar stand — the vertical pole between the base and the grip head. "Bazooka" is set slang for that riser section specifically.

> [!note]- 🔧 Cardellini clamp
> A versatile jaw clamp (invented by Steve Cardellini in 1992) that grips round, square, or flat surfaces — pipe, lumber, plywood — via a two-angled-jaw design with rubber pads that won't scratch. One of the most common clamps in a grip kit. [Cardellini Products](https://cardelliniproducts.com/cardellini-clamps/)
> ![[cardellini_2e_end_jaw_clamp_1562869405_1482661.jpg|Cardellini end jaw clamp]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Mafer clamp
> A cast-metal jaw clamp (French in origin) that bites onto pipe, stands, or set pieces up to about 2" in diameter, with a locking 5/8" baby-pin receiver on top for mounting a light, flag, or arm. Fills a similar role to the Cardellini above but with a different jaw mechanism — the two are often used interchangeably on set. [B&H: Matthews Super Mafer Clamp](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/126616-REG/Matthews_540004_Super_Mafer_Clamp.html)
> ![[Matthews_540004_Super_Mafer_Clamp_1564411793_126616.jpg|Super Mafer clamp]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Clamps (general)
> A catch-all term covering the many jaw-style mounting tools grips use to attach lights and flags to almost any surface — see the Cardellini clamp, pipe/burger clamp, and pigeon plate entries for specific types. [Cardellini Products](https://cardelliniproducts.com/)

> [!note]- 🔧 Apple box
> A sturdy wooden box (birch is common) used to level or raise something — furniture, dolly track, or an actor who needs a height boost, hence the joking nickname "Tom Cruise" (or "man-maker"). They nest inside each other in four standard sizes: full, half, quarter, and the flattest, called a pancake (1/8). [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[proaim_ab_12_nested_apple_box_set_1672939513_1734920.jpg|Nested apple box set]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Apple box orientations: New York / Chicago / LA
> Slang for how an apple box is standing: "New York" = on its narrow end (tallest), "Chicago" = on its side (medium height), "LA" = flat on the ground (shortest, most stable). Same box, three different heights depending on orientation.

> [!note]- 🔧 Paper / Ultrabounce (white border)
> "Paper" flags use a diffusion material (like Rosco's Ultrabounce) that scatters light more gently than a silk. The white border on the frame is the color code for this type, and "4x4" refers to the standard 4-foot-by-4-foot frame size.
> ![[trp_worldwide_u8_8x8_ultrabounce_1695227718_1488636.jpg|Ultrabounce frame]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Gels: CTO / CTB / CTG / minus green
> Colored plastic sheets placed over a light to shift its color temperature. **CTO** (Color Temperature Orange) warms a daylight-balanced source toward tungsten; **CTB** (Color Temperature Blue) cools tungsten toward daylight — both come in full, half, and quarter strengths for finer control. **Plus green (CTG)** and **minus green (magenta)** correct the green/magenta color spikes common in fluorescent and some LED/HMI sources. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[Rosco_RS340811_3408_Filter_RoscoSun_1490020531_44201.jpg|Rosco gel filter]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Baby LED / "1K"
> An LED fixture tuned to mimic the warm color of a 1000-watt tungsten light (a "redhead," in older terminology) while drawing far less power and running much cooler. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary — Redhead](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)

> [!note]- 🔧 Tweenie / betweenie / in-betweenie / Leko
> Open-face tungsten fixtures named for sitting *between* standard wattages — a tweenie is roughly 650W (between a 1K and a 300W "midget"), with betweenie/in-betweenie variants at lower wattages still. A related spotlight type is the **Leko** (ellipsoidal reflector spotlight), a fixture with internal shutters that can throw a hard-edged, focusable beam. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary — Leko](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)

> [!note]- 🔧 HMI
> Hydrargyrum Medium-arc Iodide — a high-intensity discharge lamp that produces daylight-balanced light (~5,600K) at much higher output per watt than tungsten, which is why they're used to punch daylight through windows or overpower the sun outdoors. Large units reach 18,000 watts (such as ARRI's M18). HMIs emit UV and can burn skin like a sunburn, so actors should bring sunscreen. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[Arri_512208_True_Blue_D12_HMI_1271859321_664954.jpg|ARRI D12 HMI]]

> [!note]- 🔧 ARRI Orbiter
> ARRI's modular LED fixture — a bare "light engine" that accepts interchangeable optics (open face, fresnel, projection/profile, soft dome) via a tool-free Quick Lighting Mount, so one head can be reconfigured for very different looks. Fully tunable color temperature via a six-color LED engine. [ARRI: introducing Orbiter](https://www.arri.com/en/company/press/press-releases-2019/arri-introduces-orbiter)
> ![[arri_l0_0034060_orbiter_open_face_60_1571837490_1507134.jpg|ARRI Orbiter open face]]

> [!note]- 🔧 C-stand
> The single most-used stand in film grip work — "C" is short for Century, the lighting company (Century Lighting, later Strand) whose name stuck even after other manufacturers took over. It's built from a collapsible three-legged base, a riser column (the "bazooka"), and a **grip head** (aka gobo head or "knuckle") that a **grip arm** slides through. The staggered leg heights let several stands nest close together around a light. A 20" C-stand is nicknamed a "Gary Coleman" for its short height. Always rig it so the load's weight tightens the knuckle rather than loosens it ("righty tighty"). [Wikipedia: C-stand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-stand) · [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[Matthews_339758_Century_C_Stand_with_1268046966_33056.jpg|Century C-stand]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Sandbag
> A heavy, saddle-shaped bag (sand or shot) draped over the tallest leg of a stand to keep it from tipping under the weight or leverage of whatever's mounted on the arm. Standard practice: never rig a stand without one. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[Matthews_299559_Sandbag_Black_1523543759_139737.jpg|Sandbag]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Combo stand
> A heavier-duty stand than a standard light stand — "combo" because its head has both a 5/8" baby pin *and* a 1-1/8" junior receiver, so it can mount either small or large fixtures. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[matthews_b366167_combo_aluminum_stand_1505841680_1046657.jpg|Combo stand]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Mombo combo
> An oversized, extra-heavy-duty combo stand, built to carry significantly more weight than a standard combo. (Matthews calls this the "Mombo Combo"; you'll also hear it called a "mambo combo.")
> ![[Matthews_377583_Mombo_Combo_Standard_Stand_1467908828_99157.jpg|Mombo combo stand]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Low boy
> A short-profile version of a combo stand for mounting lights or cameras close to the floor — same head/pin configuration as a full-height combo, just a fraction of the height.
> ![[Matthews_366066_Low_Boy_Double_Riser_1573823524_33071.jpg|Low boy stand]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Dolly & track
> The dolly is a wheeled platform the camera (and often the operator) rides on for smooth moving shots; the track is the straight or curved rail it rolls along, leveled with wedges and apple boxes. Building and leveling track is a core grip skill. [No Film School: film tool glossary](https://nofilmschool.com/film-tool-glossary)
> ![[proaim_swft_dl_swift_camera_dolly_1574187767_1504216.jpg|Camera dolly]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Jib
> A smaller, counterweighted arm (a mini-crane) that mounts on a dolly or its own base, letting the camera rise, fall, and swing on an arc — usually remote-headed rather than ridden by an operator. [No Film School: film tool glossary](https://nofilmschool.com/film-tool-glossary)
> ![[proaim_kite_11_video_camera_jib_1598533556_1579836.jpg|Camera jib]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Stinger
> Set slang for a heavy-duty extension cord, built to industry spec (12/3 gauge, Edison plugs rated well past household cords) so it can safely carry the loads lighting equipment draws. The name is said to come from the early days, when electricians connected bare wires on set. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[laird_digital_cinema_ac_12_3_25_heavy_duty_12_3_15_1563442558_1478225.jpg|Stinger extension cord]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Junior head ("lollipop")
> A large grip head sized for junior (1-1/8") pins rather than baby pins, used for mounting bigger, heavier fixtures or frames — nicknamed "lollipop" for its round, disc-like shape on a stick. A mombo combo comes with one; a standard combo stand does not. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary — Lollipop](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[Matthews_349581_Magic_Grip_Head_1359542124_139530.jpg|Junior grip head]]

> [!note]- 🔧 C-clamp
> A standard woodworking-style C-shaped clamp adapted for film use, fitted with one or two baby pins so a light or flag can be mounted directly to it — used for clamping onto set pieces, pipe, or lumber. [Cinelight: common grip equipment](https://cinelight.com/en/blog/8_common-types-of-grip-equipment.html)
> ![[Matthews_429596_C_Clamp_Baby_1308150187_33200.jpg|C-clamp with baby pin]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Gag / double gag
> A "gag" is a grip-head accessory used to sandwich two flat pieces together (like two flags, or a flag and a diffusion frame) at a shared mounting point. A "double gag" head has two wheels, letting it hold two separate arms off one stand instead of one. [Cinelight: common grip equipment](https://cinelight.com/en/blog/8_common-types-of-grip-equipment.html)
> ![[kupo_kg203911_double_gag_grip_head_1407151291_1069609.jpg|Double gag grip head]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Pipe clamp ("burger clamp")
> A clamp designed to grip pipe or scaffolding and offer a baby pin for mounting a light — "burger clamp" is set slang, likely from the way the two halves close together like a bun. [Matthews grip hardware](https://www.msegrip.com/collections/hardware)
> ![[Matthews_429618_Baby_Pipe_Clamp_1308151042_33212.jpg|Baby pipe clamp]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Pigeon plate
> A baby plate mounted flat to a piece of wood or a pancake apple box, giving you a super low, stable mounting point close to the floor or wall — grip slang for a "pigeon," i.e., something that stays low to the ground. [Kupo Grip glossary](http://blog.kupogrip.com/category/grip-glossary/)
> ![[matthews_b429085_baby_plate_6_black_1825520.jpg|Baby plate]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Chain vise grip
> A repurposed locking-pliers tool (like an Irwin Vise-Grip) modified with a length of chain, used to clamp onto pipe or truss and hang a light from above — a quick, strong overhead rigging solution. [Filmtools: grip hardware](https://2dhouse.com/products/grip-hardware)
> ![[Matthews_429039_Chain_Vice_Grip_1513272068_33160.jpg|Chain vise grip]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Grip clips
> Spring-loaded metal clamps (like oversized clothespins) numbered by jaw size — #1 (1"), #2 (2"), #3 (3") — used to pinch gel, diffusion, or small flags onto a frame or barn door quickly. [Film Speed: grip clip](https://filmspeed.com/collections/cardellini-style-clamps)
> ![[impact_ssc_a30b_steel_spring_a_clamp_1619018506_1592101.jpg|Spring grip clips]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Finger / magic finger
> A small articulating arm that adds flexibility to a baby pin mount — useful when you need to angle a light or accessory that's otherwise stuck pointing straight out. Not to be confused with a "finger" flag, a narrow flag used to block a sliver of light. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary — Finger](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[Matthews_388210_Magic_Finger_1238522891_99173.jpg|Magic finger]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Safety cable
> A steel cable looped through a rigged fixture and its mounting point as a backup, so if a clamp or pin fails, the light doesn't fall — mandatory on anything rigged overhead. It protects people and equipment from falling gear, and sets treat the rule as non-negotiable.
> ![[Matthews_6348_1_100_Safety_Cable_1255699909_634747.jpg|Safety cable]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Junior baby adapter ("butt plug")
> An adapter that steps a junior (1-1/8") receiver down to accept a baby (5/8") pin, letting a small fixture mount into a stand built for bigger gear. "Butt plug" is its standard (if blunt) set slang. [NH Grip & Electric: mounting hardware](https://www.doubledownlighting.com/grip)
> ![[Matthews_429301_Junior_Baby_Adapter_1686827145_187365.jpg|Junior baby adapter]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Blonde
> An open-face 2,000-watt (2K) tungsten fixture — sits between a redhead (1K) and the 5K units shown earlier in the wattage lineup. [No Film School: Gaffer's Dictionary](https://nofilmschool.com/the-gaffers-dictionary)
> ![[Arri_571201_Arrilite_2000_Plus_1482949319_812612.jpg|ARRI 2000 open-face light]]

> [!note]- 🔧 RED Helium sensor
> Helium is the name of RED's 8K Super 35 sensor (used in cameras like the RED Epic-W and Weapon), not a camera model on its own. RED bodies often support interchangeable lens mounts (PL, Canon EF, etc.), which is what makes it so flexible with lenses.

> [!note]- 🔧 RED Komodo-X
> A compact, global-shutter Super 35 cinema camera from RED — the global shutter eliminates the "jello" rolling-shutter distortion you can get with fast pans or vibration, which matters a lot for handheld and gimbal work. [B&H: Alexa 35 vs. Komodo-X vs. V-Raptor comparison](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/compare/ARRI_ALEXA+35_vs_RED+DIGITAL+CINEMA_Komodo+X_vs_Sony_VENICE+2+8K_vs_RED+DIGITAL+CINEMA_V-RAPTOR+XL+X/BHitems/1708577-REG_1768344-REG_1672825-REG_1807131-REG)
> ![[red_digital_cinema_komodo_x_6k_digital_cinema_1685543427_1768344.jpg|RED Komodo-X]]

> [!note]- 🔧 X-Tract / probe lens
> A "probe lens" has a long, thin barrel with the optics at the very tip, letting the camera get extremely close to (or even inside) a subject while keeping a wide field of view — the DZOFilm X-Tract is a zoom probe lens available in 0°, 90°, and 360° front-element versions, popular for macro and surreal, "impossible angle" shots. [DZOFilm X-Tract product page](https://dzofilm.com/en/products/x-tract-probe-zoom)
> ![[dzofilm_dzo_x1828a_x_tract_0_18_28mm_t8_1736923521_1871378.jpg|DZOFilm X-Tract probe lens]]

> [!note]- 🔧 RED V-Raptor
> RED's current flagship digital cinema camera, built around an 8K VistaVision (full-frame) sensor with very high frame rates and dynamic range — positioned as RED's answer to ARRI's Alexa 35. [No Film School: RED joins ARRI](https://nofilmschool.com/red-joins-arri-new-camera)
> ![[red_digital_cinema_710_0342_v_raptor_8k_vv_1631190952_1663570.jpg|RED V-Raptor]]

> [!note]- 🔧 ARRI Alexa 35
> ARRI's current flagship, a Super 35 camera built around a new sensor with roughly 17 stops of dynamic range — widely used for its color science and "film-like" highlight roll-off, and the camera RED's V-Raptor line gets benchmarked against constantly. [ARRI: Alexa 35 launch announcement](https://www.arri.com/en/company/press/press-releases-2022/arri-launches-new-alexa-35-camera)
> ![[arri_k0_0041724_alexa_35_body_1690364728_1708575.jpg|ARRI Alexa 35]]

> [!note]- 🔧 Sticks, Mitchell mount, ball head, fluid head
> "Sticks" is just set slang for a tripod. A **Mitchell mount** is the flat-plate camera-mount standard (named for the old Mitchell Camera Corporation) that most professional tripod heads and legs share, letting heads and legs from different brands mix and match. A **ball head** uses a ball-and-socket joint for quick, loose positioning — common on lighter rigs. A **fluid head** uses hydraulic damping to let an operator pan and tilt smoothly and with control, which is why it's the standard for cinema camera work over a ball head. [No Film School: film tool glossary](https://nofilmschool.com/film-tool-glossary)
> ![[Sachtler_3006_3006_Cine_30_HD_1232977361_433828.jpg|Sachtler fluid head]]
