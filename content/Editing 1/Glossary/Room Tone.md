# Room Tone

## Definition
A recording of the ambient sound of a location with no one speaking or moving — used to fill gaps left by edits (removed dialogue, cut pauses) so the background noise stays continuous instead of dropping out to dead silence at every cut.

## In Practice
Introduced in Lesson 16 alongside [[Foley]] and Score while setting up the Jacuzzi project's Audio Bin — the first formal mention of the term in the course. Grouped with Foley as one of the "first time students hear about this" audio concepts that separate a polished sound mix from a rough one; without room tone on hand, every trimmed pause in a dialogue scene risks producing an audible, distracting silence.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **The hum of an empty room you don't notice until it's gone**: every space has a subtle background presence — HVAC hum, distant traffic, a faint room hiss — and Room Tone is capturing that so an edited pause doesn't drop into dead silence that feels wrong.
- Quick demo that always lands: play a clip, then cut to true digital silence mid-scene — most students physically flinch at how wrong it sounds, which makes the case for Room Tone better than any explanation.
