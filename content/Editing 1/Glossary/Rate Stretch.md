# Rate Stretch

## Definition
A tool that changes a clip's playback speed by stretching or compressing it to fill a different duration than its native length — commonly used to slow a shot down (or speed it up) while keeping the action continuous, rather than repeating or dropping frames crudely.

## In Practice
Demonstrated in Lesson 20 as one of two hands-on applications of Murch's "Emotion first" principle: a reaction shot (Moose looking at Leroy before Don's line) that naturally lasts only about a second gets roughly doubled in length via Rate Stretch, building a stronger sense of the characters' internal struggle without adding any new footage. The lesson explicitly invites substituting a different example if a better one comes up — treated as a flexible teaching moment rather than fixed content.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Taffy being pulled**: the shot stretches out to fill more time, the same way pulled taffy gets longer without changing what it fundamentally is — the action inside the shot doesn't repeat or skip, it just plays out more slowly.
- The Moose/Leroy example is worth re-using verbatim in class: a one-second natural glance stretched to roughly two seconds is a small, almost invisible change — but it's often the difference between a beat registering emotionally and it flying by unnoticed.
