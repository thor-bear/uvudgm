# Top and Tail

## Definition
A pair of trim operations that snap an edit point to the very head (Top) or very end (Tail) of the available media — the fastest way to extend a trim all the way to a clip's natural limit.

## In Practice
Mapped in this course to **Shift+Q** (Top) and **Shift+W** (Tail) in Avid — a deliberate customization per the [[Avid Keybind Customizations]] doc that overwrites Avid's factory default mapping for those keys, so it's worth double-checking a lab machine's actual bindings before relying on muscle memory elsewhere. In Premiere, the same concept is done as a **Q / W** ripple edit. Introduced via a video of a working editor (Jennifer Roth, *Queer Eye*) praising Trim Mode and referencing Top and Tail directly, then taught hands-on later that same lesson. Reinforced in Lesson 19b as part of a live Scene 7 edit, and again in Lesson 18's Premiere trim toolkit.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Snap to the edge of the page**: like a "fit to margin" button in a word processor — instead of manually dragging a trim until it looks about right, Top/Tail snaps it exactly to where the usable material starts or ends.
- Good framing for why it's fast: it's a shortcut for "I don't want any of this, all the way to the edge" rather than trimming frame-by-frame toward a guess.
