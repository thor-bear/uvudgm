# NATO Code (Scene Lettering)

## Definition
Using NATO phonetic alphabet letters (Alpha, Beta, Charlie...) appended to a scene number on the slate to distinguish coverage variants of the same scene — "Scene 4" versus "Scene 4 Alpha" versus "Scene 4 Beta" — so audio and paperwork stay unambiguous even when a scene is covered from many different setups.

## In Practice
Taught in Lesson 17's slate call-out protocol as the standard way to layer coverage information onto a base scene/take number: "Scene 4, take one" for the first setup, then "Scene 4 Alpha, take one" for the next distinct angle. Comes up again in Lesson 25's Tom Cross interview discussion, where the *Whiplash* slate reaching "CA" (Charlie Apple) in the final scene means coverage on that scene went through the entire alphabet twice — a concrete, striking illustration of just how far this lettering convention can extend on a demanding shoot.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Sub-numbering a to-do list**: "Scene 4" is the main item, and "Scene 4 Alpha," "Scene 4 Beta" are like adding 4a, 4b underneath it — each one is clearly still part of Scene 4, but distinctly its own setup.
- The Whiplash "CA" (Charlie Apple) example does double duty here too — it's a vivid way to show how far the alphabet can actually get used up on one heavily-covered scene.
