# Timecode Sync vs. In/Out Point Sync

## Definition
Two different ways to line up separately recorded audio with picture. **Timecode sync** (a "jammed slate") matches embedded timecode values shared between camera and sound recorder. **In/Out Point sync** matches by eye or by ear at a specific reference moment — most commonly the slate clap — rather than relying on matching timecode metadata.

## In Practice
Lesson 26 walks through both, using the Fisker dialogue footage as the working example: In Points, Out Points (tail slate), and Timecode (jammed slate) are all covered as valid sync methods, with the important caveat that automatic sync via recorded camera audio only works if the camera actually records its own audio track — something the lesson notes rarely happens in narrative production with cinema-grade cameras. The lesson is explicitly flagged as unresolved/in-progress: the colleague notes uncertainty about which approach represents current professional standard practice for audio-less cinema cameras, and asks that this be verified together before teaching it as final.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Two clocks set to the same second vs. two people clapping in the same room**: Timecode sync is like two clocks that were already synchronized before recording started — they just line up automatically because their internal numbers match. In/Out Point sync is matching by ear or eye to a shared moment (the clap), the way you'd sync two phone recordings by finding the same handclap in each waveform.
