# Group Clips vs. Create Multi-Camera Source Sequence

## Definition
Two different Premiere ways to combine multiple synced camera angles of the same moment. **Create Multi-Camera Source Sequence** links the angles together while keeping each one's own metadata intact. **Group Clips** merges them together permanently, destroying that metadata in the process.

## In Practice
Lesson 26 is emphatic on this point, to the extent of including a direct personal anecdote: the colleague notes he's been "scolded" for using Group Clips, and describes it as "apparently forbidden" — a piece of real industry pushback worth passing along verbatim rather than softening into a generic best-practice tip, since it signals this isn't a stylistic preference but a genuine standard-practice mistake. Create Multi-Camera Source Sequence is the correct choice for the Fisker sync exercise and feeds directly into [[Multicam Mode]] editing afterward.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A stapled stack of photos vs. a paperclip**: Group Clips staples the footage together permanently, destroying each individual photo's own label in the process. Create Multi-Camera Source Sequence is a paperclip — it holds them together just as usefully, but you can still pull one out and see its original label anytime.
- The colleague's own "I've been scolded for using it" anecdote is worth repeating verbatim — a real, lived consequence sticks with students far better than an abstract "don't do this" rule.
