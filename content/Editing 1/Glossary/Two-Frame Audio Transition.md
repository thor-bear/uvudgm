# Two-Frame Audio Transition

## Definition
A very short (two-frame) cross-fade applied at an audio cut point specifically to eliminate the pop or click that can occur when two audio waveforms butt directly against each other with no fade at all.

## In Practice
Taught in Lesson 12 as one of the last mixing steps before a sequence is considered finished: set In/Out marks around a cut and use **Add to All** to apply the fade across every cut point at once, rather than one at a time. Comes with a practical caveat worth keeping — skip this step if the project is being handed off to dedicated audio/mix specialists afterward, since they'll likely want to work from clean, unfaded cuts rather than pre-smoothed ones.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Sanding a rough edge on a cut piece of wood**: two audio waveforms meeting with no fade is like two boards butted together with a visible seam; a two-frame fade sands that seam down so it's smooth instead of an audible click.
- Play it both ways once, live: cut two clips with a hard butt edit, let students hear the pop, then add the two-frame fade and let them hear it vanish — the before/after is far more convincing than describing it.
