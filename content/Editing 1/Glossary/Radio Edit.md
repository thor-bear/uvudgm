# Radio Edit

## Definition
An early-stage assembly of interview/dialogue material, cleaned up to work as pure audio — pauses, filler words ("errrs," "uhhhs"), and dead air removed — before any picture editing or B-Roll is layered on top. Named for the idea that it should hold together even played with the video hidden, like a radio segment.

## In Practice
Introduced in Lesson 9 as the assignment that follows Watching & Organizing Footage: with rough interviews already in the timeline, students use [[Extract]] to clean up the pauses and filler, building toward a tight audio spine before any visual work begins. Explicitly framed as expected lab-hours work rather than something taught end-to-end in class. Lesson 14's status check confirms the intended sequencing — by that point, the Radio Edit should already be in place with B-Roll added on top of it, not the other way around.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A podcast episode edited from raw interview tape**: if it would hold up as pure audio — no visuals needed — with pauses and filler tightened up, that's a Radio Edit. Picture and B-Roll get layered on top of that spine afterward, not before.
- Good test question for students: "close your eyes and just listen to your cut — does it still work?" If the answer is no, the Radio Edit isn't done yet, no matter how good the visuals look.
