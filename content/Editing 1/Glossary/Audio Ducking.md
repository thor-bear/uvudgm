# Audio Ducking

## Definition
Automatically lowering the level of one audio track (typically music) whenever another track (typically dialogue) is present, so the two don't compete — then restoring the music level once the dialogue clears.

## In Practice
Taught in Lesson 12 as part of the mixing toolkit for the Gnarly Radio Edit, alongside manual Gain/Volume adjustment — Ducking is the automated alternative to riding music levels down by hand every time an interview speaks. Comes right before the lesson's audio-replacement and pop/click-cleanup techniques ([[Two-Frame Audio Transition]]), rounding out a full pass on making a rough mix sound intentional.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A restaurant automatically turning down music when you talk to the host**: the background sound dips whenever the foreground sound (dialogue) needs the spotlight, then comes back up once it's quiet again — automatically, without someone manually riding the fader.
