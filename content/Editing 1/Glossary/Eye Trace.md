# Eye Trace

## Definition
The idea that a viewer's eye lands on a predictable point of interest within a frame, and that a cut lands most smoothly when the next shot picks up close to where the eye already was — minimizing the visual "hunt" the audience has to do after every edit. Fourth on Walter Murch's Rule of Six, behind Emotion, Story, and Rhythm.

## In Practice
Taught in Lesson 20 as part of the Rule of Six discussion, with a dedicated example clip — **"Editing Eye Trace – Oscars Montage"** — used to teach both Eye Trace itself and the related idea of cutting on motion (timing a cut to coincide with movement in frame, which naturally redirects the eye rather than fighting it).

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Handing someone a baton instead of making them run back for it**: if the next shot starts near where the viewer's eye already landed, the cut feels like a smooth handoff; if it starts somewhere totally different, the eye has to scramble to find the new point of interest.
- Try this live: freeze a frame, ask a student to point at exactly where their eye is drawn, then cut to the next shot and ask if their eye had to "jump" far to find the new focal point — that jump distance *is* Eye Trace, made visible.
