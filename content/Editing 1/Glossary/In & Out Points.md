# In & Out Points

## Definition
Markers that define a specific range of a clip or sequence — the In point marks the start of the range, the Out point marks the end. Nearly every editing operation (Overwrite, Splice-In, playing a specific range, exporting a specific range) works off whatever's currently marked.

## In Practice
Taught in Lesson 5 with a full set of Avid shortcuts: **I / O** (or **E / R**) to mark In/Out, **d** erase In, **f** erase Out, **g** erase both, **q** go to In, **w** go to Out, **6** play In to Out, and **Opt/Alt + drag** to adjust an existing point ("when I hold down Option it gives me the finger" — a line worth keeping for the mnemonic value). Premiere works the same way conceptually, though Lesson 16 flags one genuine Mac/PC divergence: deleting both In and Out points is **Option+X** on Mac but **Shift+Ctrl+X** on PC — described as the one real platform-specific exception in an otherwise consistent app.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Bookmarking a passage to read aloud**: the In point is where you'd put your finger to start reading, the Out point is where you'd stop — everything in between is what gets used.
- Think of packing tape: you don't use the whole roll every time, you pull off exactly the length you need and cut it at both ends.
## Related Terms
[[Overwrite]], [[Splice-In]] — both consume whatever's marked In-to-Out in the Source.
