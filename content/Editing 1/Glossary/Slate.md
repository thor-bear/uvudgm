# Slate

## Definition
The clapperboard used at the start (or end) of a take to visually and audibly mark scene/take information and provide a sync point for matching production audio to picture.

## In Practice
Lesson 17 covers full call-out protocol: start with a single scene/take number ("Scene 4, take one"), then add a NATO-code letter for coverage variants ("Scene 4 Alpha, take one"). The course teaches sticking to this professional discipline rather than improvised alternatives, since deviation slows the set down. Variants covered: **Soft Sticks**, **Second Sticks**, **Tail Slate**, and **MOS** (shot without sound — students are expected to know what the acronym means, not just the shorthand). Four students demonstrate each variant live. Resurfaces in Lesson 26 as the sync reference point for double-system sound — matching slate claps to production audio waveforms when auto-sync via recorded camera audio isn't available (common with cinema cameras that don't record their own sound).

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A visual and audio "label" stapled onto the front of a take**: like writing the contents on the outside of a moving box before it goes in the truck — except the slate also gives editors a single, sharp sound (the clap) that lines up perfectly on both the audio and video, which is the real reason it exists.
- The clap itself is worth demonstrating physically: show a student a waveform with an obvious spike, then explain that spike is literally the moment of the clap — it's a visual anchor point, not just a formality.
