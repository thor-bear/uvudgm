# Color Labels (Bin/Clip Color-Coding)

## Definition
Assigning a color to a clip or bin item as a visual organizing signal — which take is a select, which section of footage belongs to which speaker, which scene has been finished — read at a glance rather than by opening and checking each clip.

## In Practice
First shown in Lesson 6 for the Montage demo (coloring a clip and switching the Sequence Fast Menu to Source Color so the color is visible in the timeline), then developed further in Lesson 9 as one of three parallel footage-organization strategies alongside [[Markers]] and [[Subclips]] — Lesson 9 goes deeper into deliberate color *systems* rather than one-off coloring. Ported into Premiere in Lesson 18 as a hands-on exercise in building custom Quick Keys: color labels get mapped onto the numeric keypad (1–9 with no modifier, Shift+1–7 for the rest), and students re-color scenes using their own new keys once the preset is saved. Lesson 19a extends the same underlying tool to narrative organization — different colors meaning different things for selects during the Jacuzzi dialogue assignment.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Color-coded folders on a real desk**: red for urgent, green for done, yellow for needs review — the moment a folder's color means something specific, you can sort a giant messy pile at a glance without opening a single one.
- Warn students about the failure mode directly: an unplanned, inconsistent color scheme is worse than no color at all, because it looks organized without actually being usable — decide what each color means *before* coloring anything.
