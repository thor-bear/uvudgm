# Dip to Black

## Definition
A transition that fades the outgoing shot to black before fading up into the incoming shot, rather than dissolving directly between the two — commonly used to signal a bigger break (a scene change, a time jump) than a straight cut or simple dissolve implies.

## In Practice
Introduced in Lesson 7 as an option within the Quick Transition Tool, adjustable further in the Effects Editor and savable as a reusable template. The lesson also notes that a bin named **Custom Transitions** automatically surfaces its contents inside the Effects Palette — meaning a saved Dip to Black template becomes instantly reusable across the whole project without hunting for it. Taught in the context of deliberately restricting the Montage assignment to fade up/down only, so Dip to Black is one of the few transitions students are actually allowed to use in that assignment, while the "silly" transitions (wipes, stars) are shown mainly to be discouraged.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A chapter break in a book**: a dissolve is like a smooth paragraph transition, but Dip to Black is a hard page break with a blank page in between — it visually tells the audience "we're moving to something new," not just "time is passing."
- Good rule of thumb for students: reach for Dip to Black when the *relationship* between two moments is changing (new scene, new day, new idea), not just when two shots of the same moment need to blend.
