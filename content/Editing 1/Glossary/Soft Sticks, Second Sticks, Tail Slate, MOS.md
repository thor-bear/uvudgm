# Soft Sticks, Second Sticks, Tail Slate, MOS

## Definition
Four slate-protocol variants for specific shooting situations. **Soft Sticks** — clap the slate gently, used when the actors are already in a delicate emotional state and a loud clap would break their focus. **Second Sticks** — a second slate clap after a false start or an issue with the first. **Tail Slate** — the slate is shown at the *end* of the take (upside-down, conventionally) rather than the start, used when slating at the top would have interrupted the shot. **MOS** — shot without sound (traditionally read as "Motor Only Sync" or, per set folklore, a mangled German phrase from early Hollywood — either way, it signals no audio was recorded for that take).

## In Practice
Lesson 17 has four students demonstrate each variant live after the terminology is introduced, treating the slate not as a formality but as a real communication protocol crew members rely on. Framed alongside the course's broader emphasis on professional call-out discipline: deviating from standard terms to be cute or improvised is treated as a real distraction that slows a set down, not a harmless personal touch.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Whispering vs. shouting the same instruction**: Soft Sticks is the whispered version of a normal slate clap — same information, gentler delivery, used when a loud clap would break an actor's emotional focus.
- **A do-over marker**: Second Sticks is just "take two of the slate itself" — like re-clapping a clapperboard when the first clap didn't register cleanly, separate from the actual performance take.
- **Flipping the label to the back of the package**: a Tail Slate is shown upside-down at the end of a take instead of the start — like flipping a "before" sign to "after" once the moment it would've disrupted has already happened.
- **A silent-film convention on set**: MOS literally means no audio was recorded for that take — worth a quick historical aside, since students often assume every take has sound by default.
