# Track and Blur

## Definition
A technique that follows (tracks) a moving element across frames and applies a localized effect — usually blur, for obscuring something — so the effect stays glued to that element as it moves, rather than sitting in a fixed position on screen.

## In Practice
Taught as a matched exercise in both NLEs during Lesson 27's Effects Firehose day: in Premiere, demoed on the provided Parkour clip, blurring both sleeping men (with a note to trim the sequence clip to just the length of the jump itself, since anything longer makes the track harder to maintain); in Avid, the identical technique is repeated so students see the same underlying concept implemented in both apps. One of four required turn-in screenshots for the lesson, alongside the Psycho push and the [[AniMatte]] example.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A spotlight operator following a moving performer**: the light doesn't stay fixed on one spot on the stage — it follows the performer wherever they walk. Motion tracking works the same way, keeping the blur "glued" to the moving subject instead of sitting in one fixed patch of the frame.
- Worth demonstrating the failure mode on purpose once: apply a blur with *no* tracking to a moving subject and watch it immediately drift off them — that visible failure teaches the concept faster than a correct example alone.
