# Foley

## Definition
Custom-performed sound effects recorded to match on-screen action — footsteps, cloth movement, small physical contact sounds — recreated by a performer watching the picture, rather than pulled from a generic sound-effects library.

## In Practice
Introduced in Lesson 16 while setting up the Jacuzzi project's Audio Bin, alongside Score and [[Room Tone]] — the first time most students hear the term. The lesson deliberately states two separate reasons Foley matters, since students tend to only register the creative half on their own: it adds depth and character (the specific example given is squeaky shoes), and it's practically required for delivering a proper Music & Effects ([[M&E Track]]) track for international distribution, where dialogue gets swapped out entirely and something still needs to carry the physical sound of the scene.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A one-person orchestra of household objects**: a Foley artist watches the screen and performs footsteps, cloth rustling, and impacts live with props — often nothing like the real object (a baseball bat hitting a leather jacket for a punch, a plastic sheet and poured water for rain on an umbrella).
- Ask students to picture a boom mic on set: it's built to catch dialogue clearly, which means most physical sounds around the actor get lost or sound thin — Foley is how those sounds get put back in, deliberately and with control.
