# Gain vs. Volume

## Definition
Two related but distinct audio level controls. **Gain** is a static adjustment to a clip's base level, applied uniformly across its whole duration. **Volume** is a modulated, keyframeable adjustment layered on top — it can rise and fall over time within the same clip.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **A car's volume knob vs. cruise control**: Gain is like setting the radio to a fixed volume before you start driving — it stays put. Volume (with keyframes) is like cruise control that speeds up and slows down automatically as the road changes — it's dynamic over time.
- **Filling a glass vs. tilting it while you drink**: Gain decides how full the glass is poured in the first place; Volume is tilting it more or less as you go, moment to moment.
- **The Water Pipe Analogy (Source vs. Flow Control)**: Gain (Audio Clip Gain) is the **water pressure entering your house** at the main valve. If the source material is recorded too quiet, the water pressure coming into the pipe is just a trickle. Boosting the clip gain opens that main valve up so you actually have enough water pressure to work with. If it’s recorded too loud, it’s like a pipe about to burst (clipping/distortion), so you turn down the main valve right at the source.  Volume (Track Volume / Automation) is the **faucet handle on your sink**. Once you have healthy water pressure flowing into the house, you use the faucet handle to fine-tune how much water comes out from moment to moment while washing dishes or mixing audio.  **Key takeaway for students:** Gain fixes the incoming level from the file itself; Volume controls how much of that level flows into the final mix.
