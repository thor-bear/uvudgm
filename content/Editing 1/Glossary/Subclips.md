# Subclips

## Definition
A trimmed reference to a portion of a longer master clip, stored as its own bin item — lets an editor work with just the useful section of a long piece of footage without duplicating or permanently cutting the original media.

## In Practice
Taught in Lesson 9 as the third of three organization strategies (with bin color and [[Markers]]): create subclips from selects, arrange them in Frame View, then drag the whole set into the Timeline as a fast way to build a rough assembly. Lesson 19a gives it an honest caveat worth preserving — the colleague notes he personally uses Subclips far less in narrative editing than in non-fiction, framing it as a genuinely optional tool for some students rather than an equally essential technique in both contexts.

### How To (Media Composer)
- Load the clip into the Source monitor, then mark **In (I)** and **Out (O)** around the section to isolate.
- Make the subclip any of three ways: **Clip > Make Subclip**, **Cmd/Ctrl+U**, or right-click the Source monitor and choose **Make Subclip…**. Dragging the pop-up icon from the Source monitor's upper-left corner straight into the bin also works and skips the menu.
- The new subclip lands in the current bin, named after the master clip with an incrementing "Sub" suffix (Sub.01, Sub.02…) — rename it right away so it's identifiable later without reopening it.
## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Photocopying just the good page**: instead of carrying the entire book around every time you need that one passage, you make a copy of just that page — the original book (master clip) is untouched and still complete.
- Contrast with Markers: a Marker is a sticky note pointing *into* a long clip; a Subclip is cutting out the specific bit and giving it its own name so it behaves like a whole clip on its own.
