# Markers

## Definition
Timestamped, colorable annotations attached to a specific frame of a clip or sequence, each carrying an optional comment — used to flag a moment for later reference without altering the edit itself.

## In Practice
Taught in depth in Lesson 9 as one of three parallel footage-organization strategies (alongside bin color-coding and [[Subclips]]). In Avid, the Markers palette opens with **Shift+8** (Tools > Markers); adding a marker is **M** by default, with a customized **Add Red Marker** on **Shift+M** in this course, plus **Shift+N** / **Shift+,** to jump to the previous/next marker (all per the [[Avid Keybind Customizations]]). The course previews the Tom Cross interview specifically for his perspective on using different marker colors to mean different things — a habit worth instilling early, since an unstructured color scheme becomes noise rather than signal. Lesson 19a revisits Markers for narrative material specifically, reinforcing that the same tool serves both documentary organization (Lesson 9) and dialogue-scene organization (Lesson 19a) with different color conventions for each context.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Sticky notes in a textbook**: you're not changing the book itself, just flagging a page you'll want to find again quickly — and different colored sticky notes can mean different things (a quote to use, a good reaction, something to double-check).
- Good prompt for students: "if you had to explain this clip to someone else without them watching all of it, what would you flag and why?" — that's exactly what a marker comment is for.
