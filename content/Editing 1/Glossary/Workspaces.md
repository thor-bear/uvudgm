# Workspaces

## Definition
Saved, named window-layout arrangements suited to a particular editing task — an "Edit" workspace showing Source/Record/Timeline, an "Audio" workspace prioritizing the mixer and audio scopes, a "Color" workspace, and so on — switchable with a click or shortcut rather than manually rearranging windows each time.

## In Practice
Referenced across the Avid half of the semester: Lesson 4 has students assign their own personal Edit workspace, mapped in this course's customization to **W1 (Shift+1)** per the [[Avid Keybind Customizations]]. Lesson 12's Mixing section relies on this directly — students move into the Audio Workspace to use the Audio Mixer, then are explicitly reminded to switch back to their personal/Edit workspace afterward rather than staying parked in Audio. Bins are *not* saved as part of a workspace — only the window arrangement is. Premiere has the same underlying concept (Lesson 16's Bin/Source/Timeline window tour, Lesson 18's Shift 1–4 mouse-free window switching), though the course doesn't dwell on naming/saving custom Premiere workspaces the way it does in Avid.

## Teaching Analogies & Examples
- **Saved desktop layouts on a computer**: like switching between a "writing" desktop with just a word processor open and a "research" desktop with browser tabs and PDFs — same computer, totally different arrangement for a different task.
- Remind students explicitly what does and doesn't travel with a workspace switch: the window layout changes, but their bins stay exactly where they left them — a common early confusion is expecting bins to reset too.
