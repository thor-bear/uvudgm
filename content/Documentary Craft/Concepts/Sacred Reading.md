# Sacred Reading
> **Sacred reading** is the practice of treating a text (religious or secular) as worthy of slow, repeated, ritualized attention, so that it can speak back to your own life. In Casper ter Kuile's *The Power of Ritual*, a text becomes sacred when a community says it is, and then returns to it again and again.
>
> [[The Power of Ritual - Casper ter Kuile]]

## Insights

## What ter Kuile says in the book

- **What makes a text sacred.** He credits his mentor, Harvard Divinity School professor [[Stephanie Paulsell]], with the idea: a text is sacred when a community says so. A group returns to the same text year after year, bringing its questions, struggles and joys, and the text becomes *generative*, producing new responses in writing, music, movement, film and story. That gives us permission to infuse spiritual meaning into whatever text speaks to us. (Quoted at length in [Caitlin Kunkel's Substack post](https://inputandoutput.substack.com/p/input-sacred-reading).)
- **Not only scripture.** Sacred reading does not require religious texts. The book applies devotional practices developed for the Bible, such as giving blessings, to literature generally ([Publishers Weekly review](https://www.publishersweekly.com/978-0-06-288181-6)).
- **How to begin.** Choose a text you have read before and that meant something to you. Start with a single sentence or a small chunk, not a chapter.
- **The four stages** (adapted from lectio divina, per [Boris Smus's notes](https://smus.com/books/the-power-of-ritual-by-casper-ter-kuile/) and Kunkel):
  1. **Literal.** What is actually happening in the text? Where are we in the story?
  2. **Allegorical.** What images, stories, songs or metaphors rise up for you? Ter Kuile says to keep this stage loose and imaginative.
  3. **Personal.** What experiences from your own life does this touch?
  4. **Action.** What are you being called to do? Sometimes it is life-changing (letting go of an old hurt, taking on a responsibility), sometimes small and playful, and the link to the text need not be logical.
- **Intention, attention, repetition.** Publishers Weekly names these as the ingredients that turn ordinary activities into spiritual practice. Sacred reading is one of the practices he walks through, alongside others in the book (Sabbath as "alone time," prayer, pilgrimage, fitness, eating together).
- **Where it came from.** His podcast *Harry Potter and the Sacred Text*, co-created with Vanessa Zoltan, began after he heard her lecture on reading *Jane Eyre* as a sacred text ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Potter_and_the_Sacred_Text)).

## The lineage: where the practice comes from

### Lectio divina (Christian, monastic)
- Latin for "divine reading." The roots run through Origen (3rd c.), who saw scripture as a kind of sacrament, then Ambrose and Augustine, and Benedict of Nursia (6th c.), whose Rule gave it a place in the monastic day ([Wikipedia: Lectio Divina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lectio_Divina)).
- **Guigo II**, a Carthusian prior who died around 1188, formalized the four-step "ladder" in *Scala Claustralium* (*The Ladder of Monks*): **lectio** (read), **meditatio** (reflect, "chew" on it), **oratio** (respond in prayer), **contemplatio** (rest in silence) ([Wikipedia: Guigo II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guigo_II)). Ampleforth Abbey describes meditatio as moving the word from head to heart ([Ampleforth](https://ampleforth.org.uk/node/32)).
- Note that ter Kuile's four stages (literal / allegorical / personal / action) are an *adaptation*, not Guigo's four movements.
- Full text of *The Ladder of Monks* (PDF, short): https://nds.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/09/The-Ladder-of-Monks.pdf
- Guigo II essay recommended by HPST: [Guigo II and the Development of Lectio Divina (PDF)](https://notsorry.cdn.prismic.io/notsorry/adb72627-9b8d-42ca-b3b7-dcf8dd61c676_Guigo+II+and+the+Development+of+Lectio+Divina.pdf)

### Other practices the *Harry Potter and the Sacred Text* team draws on
From their [how-to page](https://www.harrypottersacredtext.com/resources/how-tos):
- **Havruta** (Jewish): structured paired conversation around a text, built on questions. Intro episode: [Excellence: The Dueling Club](https://www.harrypottersacredtext.com/episodes/excellence-the-dueling-club). Background: [My Jewish Learning](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/havruta-learning-in-pairs/). The Talmud says two scholars sharpen one another (Ta'anit 7a).
- **Florilegia** (monastic): a quote journal whose collected passages you then read as their own text. They learned it from Stephanie Paulsell. Episode: [Love: Dobby's Reward](https://www.harrypottersacredtext.com/episodes/love-dobbys-reward). The word means "gathering of flowers" (flos + legere), and it overlaps with the commonplace-book tradition.
- **Sacred Imagination** (Ignatian): imagine yourself into the scene using the five senses. Episode: [Vulnerability: The Sorting Hat](https://www.harrypottersacredtext.com/episodes/vulnerability-the-sorting-hat). Also [Praying with Scripture (Ignatian Spirituality)](https://www.ignatianspirituality.com/ignatian-prayer/the-what-how-why-of-prayer/praying-with-scripture).
- **PaRDeS** (medieval Jewish, four-level): "orchard"; pluck any sentence like fruit and be nourished. Episode: [Owl Post and Rabbi Scott Perlo](https://www.harrypottersacredtext.com/episodes/special-edition-owl-post-and-rabbi-scott-perlo).
- **Marginalia**: your responses to the text matter as much as the text, as in the Talmud's layered commentary. Episode: [Belief: Dudley Demented](https://www.harrypottersacredtext.com/episodes/belief-dudley-demented).
- **Four Reliances** (Buddhist): guidelines for finding meaning beyond the literal. [Rigpa Wiki overview](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Four_reliances). Episode: [Earnestness: The Scar](https://www.harrypottersacredtext.com/episodes/earnestness-the-scar).

### HPST's three-part definition of "sacred"
([Sacred Text page](https://www.harrypottersacredtext.com/resources/sacred-text))
1. **Trusting the text**: it is more than "just entertainment," though not perfect.
2. **Rigor and ritual**: slow, repeated, concentrated reading is what makes it sacred.
3. **Reading in community**.

## People
- **Casper ter Kuile**: author; Harvard Divinity (M.Div.) and Kennedy School graduate; co-founder of [Sacred Design Lab](https://www.sacred.design) and The Nearness; co-author of *How We Gather*. Site: [caspertk.com](https://www.caspertk.com).
- **[[Stephanie Paulsell]]**: his mentor; Harvard Divinity School professor (retired December 2024) whose work centers on the spiritually formative practices of reading and writing. Author of *Honoring the Body*, *Religion Around Virginia Woolf*. [HDS profile](https://www.hds.harvard.edu/people/stephanie-paulsell). Her 2024 retirement lecture: [Lost in the Mystery of God](https://www.hds.harvard.edu/news/2024/10/22/video-lost-mystery-god-remembered-wisdom-retirement). Her essays: ["Reading St. Therese"](https://bulletin.hds.harvard.edu/reading-st-therese/), ["Devotion in the Study of Religion"](https://bulletin.hds.harvard.edu/devotion-in-the-study-of-religion/).
- **Vanessa Zoltan**: atheist chaplain, co-creator of HPST. Her lecture on *Jane Eyre* seeded the podcast. Book: *Praying with Jane Eyre: Reflections on Reading as a Sacred Practice* (2021), which ends with a guide to sacred reading any text.
- **Guigo II**: Carthusian prior, author of *Scala Claustralium*.
